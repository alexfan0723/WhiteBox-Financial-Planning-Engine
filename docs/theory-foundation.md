# Theory Foundation

WhiteBox starts from the view that household financial planning is a structured balance-sheet and lifecycle decision problem.

A planning system should not only answer "can the client afford this?" It should identify which facts are confirmed, which assumptions are hypothetical, which constraints bind, and which outcomes remain sensitive to market, income, liquidity, insurance, property, and family responsibility conditions.

## 1. Household Balance Sheet

WhiteBox treats the household as the planning unit.

The model should distinguish:

- liquid assets
- investment assets
- owner-occupied property
- investment property
- insurance-related value and protection
- short-term liabilities
- long-term liabilities
- known commitments
- future planning goals

The purpose is not just accounting. The balance sheet is the base for solvency, liquidity, resilience, and planning capacity.

## 2. Lifecycle Finance

Households make decisions across time.

WhiteBox modeling considers:

- accumulation stage
- child education stage
- mortgage and property stage
- retirement transition
- retirement income drawdown
- intergenerational responsibility
- protection and contingency planning

Planning output should make the time dimension explicit. A decision that looks safe today may fail under retirement timing, income interruption, or liquidity stress.

## 3. Cash-Flow and Liability Matching

A household plan depends on whether future cash inflows can support future obligations.

WhiteBox separates:

- current income
- recurring living expense
- financing expense
- insurance premiums
- education funding
- retirement spending
- passive income
- one-time transactions
- liability repayment

This supports explicit matching between cash-flow sources and future responsibilities.

## 4. Risk and Sustainability

WhiteBox uses long-horizon risk analysis to evaluate whether a plan remains viable under uncertainty.

Relevant stress dimensions include:

- market return variation
- income interruption
- expense growth
- property liquidity
- retirement timing
- family responsibility
- insurance protection gaps
- debt service pressure

The model should explain not only an expected result, but also failure modes and boundary cases.

## 5. Insurance and Protection Gap Analysis

Insurance is treated as a protection and planning instrument, not merely as an expense line.

WhiteBox should distinguish:

- current policy facts
- protection coverage
- cash value or surrender value when relevant
- premium commitments
- estimated planning value
- formal contract-backed value
- hypothetical future purchase scenarios

This prevents double counting and prevents estimated or conceptual values from being presented as formal contract facts.

## 6. Mainland China Context

WhiteBox is designed for mainland China, where planning assumptions often differ from imported templates.

Important local dimensions include:

- property-heavy household wealth
- mortgage and housing affordability
- family responsibility and elder support
- education funding expectations
- local insurance product design
- advisor-mediated planning workflows
- liquidity constraints under property concentration
- retirement income uncertainty

WhiteBox aims to encode these assumptions explicitly rather than hiding them inside generic planning language.

## 7. Human Expert Review

WhiteBox is not meant to replace professional judgment.

The system should produce traceable calculations, structured scenarios, detector evidence, and advisor-readable explanations. Domain experts remain important for market assumptions, suitability review, client communication, and final planning judgment.
